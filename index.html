Mathematics · Calculus Final answer hidden

"Find the derivative of f(x) = (3x² + 5) / (2x - 1). How do I apply the quotient rule without mixing up terms?"

1

Step 1: Identify Key Formula & Components

Unlocked

Notice you have a fraction \(\frac{u(x)}{v(x)}\). The Quotient Rule states: f'(x) = [ u'(x) · v(x) − u(x) · v'(x) ] / [ v(x) ]² A handy memory trick: "Low d-High minus High d-Low, over the square of what's below!"

2

Step 2: Differentiate Each Part Separately

Unlocked

Break it into sub-tasks before combining:

  • Top function \(u(x) = 3x^2 + 5\) → Derivative \(u'(x) = 6x\)
  • Bottom function \(v(x) = 2x - 1\) → Derivative \(v'(x) = 2\)
  • Bottom squared \([v(x)]^2 = (2x - 1)^2\)
3

Step 3: Assemble & Expand the Numerator

Guided Checkpoint

Substitute into the formula: Numerator = (6x)(2x - 1) − (3x² + 5)(2) Multiply out both terms carefully:
• Term 1: \(6x \times 2x = 12x^2\) and \(6x \times (-1) = -6x\)
• Term 2: \(2 \times 3x^2 = 6x^2\) and \(2 \times 5 = 10\). Don't forget to distribute the negative sign!

4

Step 4: Your Turn! (The Final Step)

Earn +50 Bonus Pts

Subtract \((6x^2 + 10)\) from \((12x^2 - 6x)\). Combine like terms for the numerator, and place it over \((2x - 1)^2\).
